## Methodology and verification standard

I prioritized **current official supplier websites and product pages**, followed by producer/mill contact pages, government or industry sources, recognized Zimbabwean supplier associations, and then business directories and marketplaces. Official evidence included GrainCo, AgriSwiss/RSAgri, Surface Wilmar, PHI Commodities, United Refineries, GMB, Mambo, Sebakwe Milling, Capital Foods, G&W, Rock Chemical Fillers and Incyte. citeturn31search2turn39search0turn47search4turn30search5turn46view3turn36search1turn36search6turn32search2turn47search7turn33search3turn34search1turn46view2

I then widened the search to smaller and less formal businesses through sources such as AgriUniverse, ZimbabweYP, Global Suppliers Online, ZimPlaza and other Zimbabwe-focused listings. This was important because an “exhaust Zimbabwe” exercise would miss a significant portion of smaller commodity traders if it relied only on companies with sophisticated websites. Examples include Falcon Foods, Netgrain, Zone Network Foods, Grain Solutions, Croesus Synedgy and Partner Commodities. citeturn39search5turn41search0turn31search7turn31search6turn41search5turn41search6

I treated **product verification separately from company verification**. Mega Market, for example, is unquestionably an operating Mutare flour mill with an official contact number and a stated wheat-processing capacity of 300 tonnes/day, but its public material does **not** explicitly say that it currently sells wheat bran to third parties. It therefore appears in the CSV because it is an excellent direct-mill prospect, but internally it remains an *unverified wheat-bran sales lead* until you call. citeturn38search0turn38search1 The same caution applies to National Foods: it has major milling operations and verifiable Harare/Bulawayo contacts, but I did not find a current official raw-bulk wheat-bran sales page. citeturn38search3

Where I could not substantiate a phone number or email, I used **“unspecified”**. I have not generated phone numbers from number ranges, guessed email formats, or assumed that a contact for a parent company is automatically the right ingredient-sales contact.

## Province coverage and sourcing gaps

The supplier universe is **not evenly distributed around Zimbabwe**. Harare dominates the directory because many importers, commodity traders, animal-feed companies, oilseed processors and head offices are concentrated there. Bulawayo is the strongest second hub, particularly because **AgriSwiss/RSAgri and United Refineries** provide meaningful upstream opportunities rather than merely retail branches. citeturn39search0turn43search2

Outside the two metropolitan centres, the most commercially important finds are **Mega Market in Mutare for the wheat-bran investigation**, **Sebakwe Milling in Kwekwe for sunflower cake and sorghum**, **RCF at Chegutu for feed limestone**, and **G&W's Rushinga limestone operation**. citeturn38search1turn32search3turn34search2turn33search3 This is important for FeedSport because sourcing directly from the processor can produce a very different cost structure from purchasing the same ingredient from a Harare reseller.

Capital Foods' current distribution list demonstrates that formal feed-sector distribution reaches well beyond those main hubs, with current listed outlets/agents in places including Bulawayo, Chegutu, Gweru, Kwekwe, Mutare, Zvishavane, Gutu, Jerera, Mt Darwin, Mvurwi, Rusape, Nyanga and Ntabazinduna. citeturn47search1 That is useful for local availability, but **an agent selling an ingredient is not necessarily a new upstream supplier**. For FeedSport's buying strategy, I would therefore continue to prioritize the producer or national commodity desk rather than counting every branch as an independent supply source.

The biggest remaining intelligence gap is consequently not Harare—it is **finding independent millers/processors in Masvingo, Matabeleland North and Matabeleland South who will sell one or more of these six ingredients directly**. I deliberately did not populate those gaps with generic stockfeed shops that only sell finished rations or with businesses whose product relationship could not be substantiated. That makes this list somewhat smaller than a raw Google-directory dump but substantially more useful for procurement.
